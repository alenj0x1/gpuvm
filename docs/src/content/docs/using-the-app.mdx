## The window

- **Sidebar:** **Home**, **Library** and [**Agents**](/docs/agents/) at the top, then every Hyper-V VM on the PC (created with GpuVM or not) under the [space](#spaces) it belongs to, and **Create machine** at the end. **Settings** sits at the foot. Each machine shows its state as a dot on its icon and, on the right, what the dot doesn't say: a tiny chart of its last minute (processor, memory and GPU in their colors) while it runs, or the step of a job in progress.
- **Resize or fold it:** drag the line on its edge to change its width; click the line, or drag it narrow, to fold it.
- **Reorder by dragging:** drag a machine to another place, in its space or in another one. The others make room as it passes and it shows faded where it would land. Spaces are reordered the same way, by dragging their name.

GpuVM opens without administrator rights; the **GpuVM** Windows service does the Hyper-V work for it. If the service is stopped and set to **Manual**, the app says so with **Start service**. If the service is missing, it says so with **Repair**, which registers it again after a UAC prompt. Deleting a machine, a disk, a shared disk or a template asks for a UAC confirmation first; if you say no, nothing is deleted. If Hyper-V can't be read, a notice shows the error with **Retry**.

The interface is in English and Spanish: it follows Windows' display language, or the one chosen in [Settings](#settings).

## Home

The app opens on **Home**, which fits the window without scrolling:

- **The stage:** the machine in use (or the last one opened) inside its cube, with the other machines of the [space](#spaces) beside it, on the space's world. Drag to turn it, scroll to zoom.
- **Beside the stage:** that machine's processor, memory and GPU use, **Free memory for machines** (how much the PC has free and which machines fit), **View machine** and its main action (**Open**, **Start and open**…). A machine that doesn't fit offers to open with the memory that does fit, for example **Open with 6 GB**: it lowers its memory to what fits (saved, like changing it on its page) and opens it.
- **Below:** every other machine of the space with its action (the row scrolls sideways, also by dragging it), the **Library** at a glance (how many profiles, templates, shared disks and ISOs) with **Create machine**, and what needs attention, only when there is something: a GPU driver out of date, the viewer not paired, apps waiting to be installed, the Windows password missing or little disk space left, each with the button that fixes it.

Drag the lines between these parts to resize them (double-click one to reset it); the sizes are remembered.

With no machines yet, Home shows an empty machine in the cube with what the Library already has ready and **Create machine**.

## Spaces

A space groups machines by what they are for (work, games, AI tests…). Home shows one space at a time, picked from the tabs over the stage (they scroll with the wheel); the sidebar lists the machines under their space. The first space is **My machines**.

- **New space** (the **+** after the tabs) and the pencil next to them (to edit the space shown) open the same dialog: **Name**, **Ambient**, **Time of day** and the **Machines** in it. **Delete space** sends its machines back to another space.
- **Ambient** is the world drawn around the cubes: **Motherboard** (chips, capacitors, connectors and traces with data running in the accent color, with the space's and machine's names printed on the board), **Blocks** (a baseplate of studs with paths, a pond, trees, houses and brick figures, by **Day**, **Evening**, **Night** or **By the clock**) or **Studio** (the app's plain gray). The world turns and zooms with the cube, has a light that shades every piece and casts shadows, and the hologram lights the floor under each machine.
- A new machine goes into the space it is created from. A machine moves to another space from its **Customize** section, or by dragging it from the sidebar or from Home's cards onto another space.
- A space with no machines offers a **New machine** card and the machines of other spaces, with **Bring here**.

<Shot name="home-board" alt="Home on a space with the Motherboard world: chips and traces around the machine's cube" />

## Games

**Play**, over Home's stage when you point at it, opens a gallery of games. Each card shows the game playing on its own, with your best score. Before playing, a screen shows the game with the machine and mode you pick, its controls drawn as keys and mouse buttons, a few tips and your record.

- **Overclock:** your machine flies through bricks and rival machines. Move with **W A S D**, aim with the mouse, shoot with the left button or **Space**, and use the ability with the right button or **E**; **P** pauses. Stickers bring weapons, a shield or a life; taking things down fills a frenzy where your figures come out huge. Every 40 seconds you cross into another zone (the studio, a motherboard, a world of blocks). Modes: **Solo**, or **Team**, where your other machines fly with you and shoot on their own against more and tougher rivals (it needs at least two machines).
- **Bit by bit:** your machine jumps through the gaps between columns (left button, **Space** or **↑**), in the **Studio**, on a **Motherboard** or among **Blocks**. Every gap scores, bits count double, and the gaps narrow as your score grows.
- **Stack:** a block slides over the tower and one click (or **Space** or **↓**) drops it; what hangs over falls off and the tower narrows. Right on top is a Perfect, worth double, and three in a row grow the block back. Your machine is the base and its figure rides the top. Every 8 floors the tower climbs into another world (blocks, a motherboard, the studio, then space) and it gets harder.
- **Cache:** brick figures rain down; move your machine along the floor with the mouse (or **A** / **D**) to catch them. Each one that reaches the floor costs a heart. Golden ones are worth five, spiders cost a heart if you catch them, and stickers bring a clone that catches beside you, a wider catcher, slow time, a magnet, a shield or a heart.

Each game and mode keeps its best score.

## Create a machine

**Create machine** (in the sidebar, on Home or from a profile in the Library) opens one large dialog: its sections on the left, a summary always in view on the right, and the cube beside it, built as you choose.

<Shot name="create" alt="Create machine dialog with its sections on the left, the profiles and a summary with the machine's cube" />

<Steps>

1. **Name and space:** letters, numbers, `_` or hyphens, up to 40 characters, no spaces (it suggests `win-gpu`), and the space it goes into. While you type the name, the camera goes to the board's serial plate and engraves it letter by letter.

2. **Starting point:** **Install Windows**, **From template** (when you have [templates](#templates)) or **From a file** (a machine [exported](#export-and-import) as `.gpuvm`), and a profile: **Balanced** (`balanced`), **Maximum performance** (`max-performance`, the default) or one of your own. The profile fills in everything below, including its ISO, apps and use; see [Profiles](/docs/profiles/). From a template the machine is ready in seconds and only needs the name; Windows, its apps, cleanup level and password come from the template.

3. **Resources:** threads, memory, GPU share and disk. The disk stops at what fits on the drive once every machine's disk there is full, keeping 20 GB for Windows.

4. **Windows and account:** the **Windows image** (the ISOs you already used or added first, then the ones in your Downloads and Desktop folders, or **Find another ISO**), the **User** (`gpuvm` by default; up to 20 letters, numbers, dots or hyphens, names Windows reserves are rejected) and the **Password**, at least 6 characters, typed twice.

5. **Apps**, installed the first time the machine opens ([Apps](#apps)); **Cleanup**, how clean Windows is ([Windows cleanup](#windows-cleanup)); and **Customize**, its decoration ([Customize](#customize)), with **Random look** to fill it in one click.

6. Click **Create machine**. It stays disabled until the form is complete; any problem shows next to its field as you type. If you changed what the profile filled in, **Save as profile** keeps it as a new profile.

</Steps>

The dialog then shows each step as it happens. You can close it: the progress stays in the sidebar, and if creation fails the dialog opens again with the error. When it's done the new machine opens and starts; Windows finishes setting itself up on its own in a few minutes.

## The machine page

Click a machine in the sidebar (or **View machine** on Home) to open its page:

- **Header:** a banner that moves with the GPU, flickers with the processor and fills with memory; the machine's icon, name and state; its Windows edition, GPU and profile as small labeled pieces; the clipboard button ([Paste in the VM](#paste-in-the-vm)), the **⋯** menu ([More options](#more-options)) and the main button ([Start and open](#start-and-open)).
- **Usage strip:** **Processor** (threads and % in use), **Memory** (fixed memory and how much is in use), **GPU** (% in use while running, or the share assigned while off, and the GPU's name) and **Uptime** (and the machine's IP address), each with a small chart of the last five minutes.
- **Sections:** **Machine**, **Picture and control**, **Windows** and **Customize**, on one page. The bar at the top marks the section in view and takes you to the others. Switching to another machine keeps the same section and place.

<Shot name="machine" alt="A machine's page: the banner and header, the usage strip, the section bar, the Machine options and the cube beside them" />

### The cube

Beside the options, always in view, the machine is a hologram inside a glass cube: motherboard, processor (one cell per thread), memory (one module per 4 GB), graphics card (its GPU share as a strip of lights) and disk, the apps floating around and its screen projected above. Lightning reaches each piece as often as it works; what the cleanup keeps out bounces off the glass.

- Drag to turn it any way, scroll to zoom where the mouse is, drag with the wheel pressed to move it, double-click to go back.
- It follows what you change before saving: the camera closes in on the piece you change and then eases back, and a profile that changes several at once runs through them while the hologram rebuilds.
- The other machines of its space are drawn small under it; left alone for a while, the machine floats and joins them.
- Drag the edge of the cube's panel to make it wider (it never squeezes the options, and it is remembered).

## Start and open

The main button changes with the machine's state:

| State | Button | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Off | **Start and open** | Starts the machine and opens it in GpuVM's viewer. |
| On | **Open** | Opens it in GpuVM's viewer. |
| Paused | **Resume** | Resumes the machine. |
| Off, not enough free memory | **Open with N GB** / **Not enough memory** | Opens with the memory that fits / says what is missing. |
| Not created with GpuVM | **Start** / **View console** | Starts it / opens the Hyper-V console. |

**Start and open** leaves everything ready on its own, and the button shows each step while it works:

1. If the machine is off and the host's GPU driver changed, it copies the new driver into it first (avoids a black screen or error code 43).
2. Starts the machine and waits for Windows (the first time, Windows finishes its setup) to start and sign in on its own. If Windows is finishing updates, or restarts to finish them, the button says so.
3. Returns the Windows session to the GPU display if it was taken somewhere else.
4. Sets the machine's display to the picture's resolution and frame rate, and makes Sunshine capture it.
5. Installs gamepad support and the administrator prompt watcher the first time.
6. Pairs the viewer with Sunshine if this PC isn't paired yet.
7. Opens the viewer and waits until the picture shows.

Work that lasts until Windows restarts (the display, the capture, the sound) is done once per boot, through one connection to the machine. Opening a machine that is already on takes about 7 to 10 seconds until you see the picture; from off, about a minute, most of it Windows and Sunshine starting. If the machine is off, saved or paused while GpuVM waits for its network address, it says so at once instead of waiting.

Warnings along the way (for example, audio not configured yet) appear as notifications.

## The machine's picture

While the machine connects, its cube moves to the center with the steps. Then the picture fills the window under the title bar, or goes full screen if the machine is set to.

- **Home** in the title bar, or **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H**, goes back to Home and keeps the machine connected: **Open** goes straight back to its picture. To disconnect it instead, see [Settings](#settings).
- **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G** opens the side panel beside the picture: a board of widgets (machines, actions, the picture's numbers, a clock, the games, the weather) that **Edit** arranges. See [Side panel](/docs/streaming/#side-panel).
- While a picture is shown, the app's holograms and scenes stop, so the GPU goes to the machines.

## Keyboard, mouse and gamepads

With the machine in full screen, **Alt+F4**, **Alt+Tab** and the **Windows key** go to the machine instead of closing the picture or taking you back to this PC. To quit the stream, press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Q**. With the mouse **For games**, **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Z** releases it without closing.

Gamepads work too: GpuVM installs the virtual gamepad driver (ViGEmBus) in the machine, and a controller connected to this PC reaches Windows in the machine as an Xbox 360 controller.

From a key, click, mouse move or gamepad button on this PC to the change in the picture takes about 17 ms (median) at 120 FPS or more, about 11 ms more than the same PC without streaming. At 60 FPS it adds one more frame.

## Administrator prompts

Windows draws its administrator prompts (UAC) on a separate, protected desktop that can't be streamed, so GpuVM shows them itself. While the machine's picture is shown, the prompt drops from the top of the picture, which darkens as on Windows' own secure desktop; for a machine whose picture isn't shown, a small card appears over everything. Both show the machine, the program asking and its publisher (**Unknown publisher** when it isn't signed), and **Allow** and **Deny** answer it in the machine.

- Only this PC can answer: a program inside the machine can't approve itself.
- If a different prompt opened in the meantime, nothing is pressed and the new one shows.
- It needs the machine's [Windows password](#windows-password).
- After quitting GpuVM with machines left on, a tiny notices-only mode keeps showing their prompts (**Settings → Window → Administrator prompts after quitting**).

## Paste in the VM

While the machine is running, the clipboard button next to the **⋯** menu, **Paste in the VM**, copies the text you copied on this PC to the machine's clipboard, ready for Ctrl+V. It only goes from the PC to the machine: the machine never reads this PC's clipboard. It needs the [Windows password](#windows-password).

## Files into a machine

Drop files or folders on the machine's picture to copy them into the **Received** folder on its desktop (1 GB in about 25 seconds). The **Files** widget of the [side panel](/docs/streaming/#side-panel) shows the copies in progress and, with files selected in the machine's File Explorer, **Send what is selected to** another machine.

## Clipboard

The **Clipboard** widget keeps the last 8 things you copied on this PC (text, an image or files), ready to paste into the machine with a click; **On copy** sends each new copy on its own. They live only in memory, while a machine is open, and private copies that password managers mark are skipped. Nothing is read from the machine's clipboard.

## Captures

The **Capture** widget and these shortcuts work on the machine's picture:

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P** | Takes a screenshot. |
| **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R** | Starts or stops recording. |
| **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B** | Saves the last 30 seconds. |

They are saved without re-encoding, in `Pictures\GpuVM\<machine>` and `Videos\GpuVM\<machine>`. **Settings → Captures** changes the folders and turns on **Save the last 30 s** (off by default: the viewer keeps those seconds in memory, about 130 MB at 35 Mb/s).

## Status notch

Recording, saved captures, you took control, an agent paused or working: status notices show in one notch at the bottom center of the picture, stacked. **Settings → Appearance → Status notices** moves it to the top center, a top corner, or only the side panel. Requests that need an answer always drop from the top.

## Agents

AI agents, such as Claude Code, can use machines made for them while you watch and take control. See [Agents](/docs/agents/).

## Machine

The **Machine** section, for machines created with GpuVM:

- **Processor:** threads, from 2 up to the host's threads minus one; it says how many are left for your PC.
- **Memory:** GB, from 4 up to the host's memory minus 4 GB, all reserved when the machine starts.
- **GPU for the VM:** 10–100 %, in steps of 10.
- **Disk:** grows the disk (it can't shrink). A dynamic disk grows as the machine writes, so the slider stops at what fits once every machine's disk on that drive is full, keeping 20 GB for Windows; the bar below shows this machine's disk, the room reserved for the others and what would be left. A machine built on a template has the template's size. Windows' C: drive grows on its own the next time it opens.
- **Network:**
  - **Internet only** (the default): the machine reaches neither this PC nor your local network, except for what the stream needs. The rules live on the machine's virtual switch port, outside it, so an infected machine can't remove them.
  - **Internet and local network**: the machine reaches this PC and the rest of your network. Use it only when you need it, for example for a NAS.
- **Shared disks** (when you have one): each disk with what it is for, how full it is and where it is connected. See [Shared disks](#shared-disks).

Processor, memory, GPU and disk need the machine off: if it's running the button reads **Shut down and apply**, which validates, shuts it down, applies and starts it again. If it can't start again (for example, not enough free memory), the previous settings are restored. **Discard** drops what you changed.

### Restore points

**Restore points** saves Windows as it is now (**Create point**, or **Shut down and create point** if it's running) to go back to that moment if something goes wrong. **Go back** returns Windows to how it was then, with its apps and files, in about a second; a point of how it is now is created first, in case you need it. The machine's settings don't change. Hyper-V can't checkpoint machines with a GPU, so GpuVM uses its own points.

A [disposable machine](#disposable-machines) shows **Disposable machine** here instead, with **Keep** to make it a regular machine.

## Picture and control

How the viewer shows the machine and how you control it:

<Shot name="options" alt="The Picture and control section: use, frame rate, vertical sync, frame pacing, mouse, resolution and window" />

- **Use:** what the machine is for. It heads its group and sets the four options under it; changing one by hand switches it to **Custom**.

  | Use | Frames per second | Vertical sync | Frame pacing | Mouse |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Games** | Monitor | Whole picture | Instant | For games |
  | **Movies and video** | 60 FPS | Whole picture | Even | Free |
  | **Work** | Monitor | Whole picture | Instant | Free |

  **Agent** makes it a machine for AI agents: a 1280x800 picture, recorded while an agent works, on an isolated network, with **Agents' Windows user** under it. See [Agents](/docs/agents/#machines-for-agents).

  - **Frames per second:** offered for the monitor the machine opens on: its own rate (**Monitor**, read every time it opens; GpuVM adds that rate to the machine's virtual display when it lacks it), the rates that keep even with it, the ones that may stutter, and a custom value from 10 to 480. At the monitor's own rate V-Sync adds no latency.
  - **Vertical sync:** **Whole picture** shows every frame complete on the monitor; **Faster** shows each frame as soon as it arrives, even if the monitor is halfway through drawing the previous one (tearing).
  - **Frame pacing:** **Even** spreads the frames at a steady rhythm, best at 60 FPS; **Instant** shows each frame as soon as it's ready. Only with the whole picture.
  - **Mouse:** **For games** (the machine takes the mouse, as games need to turn the camera) or **Free** (the pointer moves in and out of the window as in remote desktop; most games don't work with it).
- **Resolution:** 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 2160p.
- **Window:** **Full screen**, or **Window** (the picture stays inside GpuVM's window, under the title bar).
- **Open on** (with more than one monitor): **As in Settings** follows the choice in [Settings](#settings); Windows' main monitor, whichever it is at the time; or a monitor by its name. A chosen monitor that isn't connected falls back to the main one.
- **Advanced** (click to show):
  - **Codec:** **Automatic** (the viewer picks the best one this PC can show), H.264, HEVC or AV1. If the picture doesn't show, go back to **Automatic**.
  - **Picture quality:** the bitrate in Mbps, up to 150. Higher uses more network; at the minimum (**Automatic**), the viewer picks it from the resolution and frame rate.

These are saved without stopping the machine: the picture settings apply the next time you open it, and the network right away.

## Windows

### Windows cleanup

How clean Windows is inside the machine. Each level includes the previous ones:

- **Normal**: Windows as it comes.
- **Privacy**: diagnostic data at the minimum the edition allows (on Pro, Windows doesn't go below "Required"), no advertising ID, activity history, suggestions, Spotlight, Bing in Start, Copilot or Recall.
- **No extra apps**: also removes preinstalled apps (News, Weather, Solitaire, Clipchamp, personal Teams, the new Outlook...), also for new users, plus OneDrive and widgets. They can be installed again from Microsoft Store.
- **Minimal**: also turns off the telemetry and error reporting services and services a machine doesn't use (maps, location, fax, demo mode), and keeps Store apps from running in the background.

Next to the levels, a list of everything Windows comes with (tracking, ads, Bing, Copilot, preinstalled apps, OneDrive, widgets, background services) says what each one does now or, when the chosen level removes it, how Windows ends up without it, beside a scene where those pieces stop reaching Windows. Choose a level and click **Apply**, or **Start and apply** if the machine is off. A lower level undoes the settings of the higher ones. Microsoft Defender, Windows Update, SmartScreen, UAC, the firewall, Microsoft Store and Xbox are never touched. The level can also be chosen when creating the machine or in a profile; it is applied on Windows' first sign-in.

### Apps

Game launchers (Steam, Epic Games, Battle.net, EA app, GOG Galaxy, Ubisoft Connect), browsers (Chrome, Brave, Firefox), media (VLC, OBS Studio, Spotify), Discord, 7-Zip, WinRAR and Proton VPN, installed in the machine:

- The catalog sits on the left by category, as **Tiles** (pick several, then **Install** in the bar that counts them) or as a **List** (**Install** per app); the choice is remembered. Installed apps are marked. **Start and install** starts the machine first.
- On the right, the search and **On this machine**: the apps already installed, each with a menu (or right-click) for **Reinstall** and **Uninstall**. Uninstalling runs the app's own uninstaller and asks first; Battle.net's opens in the machine's window to finish there.

Each installer is downloaded inside the machine from its official source and only runs if Windows confirms it is signed by its publisher; pinned versions are also checked by SHA-256. Apps can also be picked when creating the machine; they are installed the first time it opens. The list of installed apps is checked each time the page opens with the machine running, and remembered while it is off. It needs the [Windows password](#windows-password).

## Customize

Decorates the machine's cube; each machine keeps its decoration, also when renamed, and it shows on Home too.

<Shot name="personalize" alt="The Customize section with the space, color, LED sign and stickers, and the cube with its fixed views panel" />

- **Space:** the space the machine is in (choosing another moves it there, or **New space** creates one with it), and **View space** to see it on Home.
- **Random:** **Random look** picks a color, sign, stickers and figures in one click; keep changing whatever you like.
- **Color:** the hologram, the lightning and the machine's icon. With none picked, it uses the accent color.
- **LED sign:** text that runs around the glass (**Off** / **On**). **Text** (empty shows the machine's name), **Sign color** (amber, red, green, white or the machine's color), **Height** (also by dragging it up or down on the cube) and **Live usage**: **Text only** or **With usage** (GPU and processor use while it runs).
- **Stickers:** GpuVM, star, bolt, heart, cat, dog, spider, ghost, rocket, fire, diamond, moon, controller, or **Upload image** for one of your own (PNG, WEBP, JPG or GIF, up to 8 MB). Drag them onto any side of the glass.
- **Figures:** toy-brick figures that stand on top of the cube (lucky cat, plant, duckling, robot, cactus, mushrooms, controller, mug, penguin, rocket, bonsai, dog, cat, spider, magic cube, bunny, bear, unicorn, tiger, fawn).

On the cube, while this section is in view:

- Dragging shows the face's cells lit in the accent and things snap to them. Hold the right button and drag sideways to turn a sticker or figure (it settles every 15°); a right-click opens its menu (turn, size, duplicate, copy, cut, paste, remove).
- Pick several with Shift or Ctrl and click, a box drawn with Shift on empty space, or Ctrl+A; they move, turn, duplicate (Ctrl+D) and are removed (Delete) together, and the arrows move them one cell at a time. What is picked shows under a soft layer of the accent.
- Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste stickers and figures, also from one machine to another.
- A panel on the cube shows it **Free** or from the **Front**, **Right**, **Back**, **Left** or **Top**, and keeps it still there.

## Windows password

If GpuVM doesn't have the machine's Windows password (for example, after rebuilding its state with `repair`), the page shows a card asking for it, for the machine's user. It is saved encrypted with DPAPI and lets GpuVM fix things inside the machine through PowerShell Direct (without network and without asking you): waiting for setup to finish, moving the session back to the GPU display, setting the display resolution, installing gamepad support and answering administrator prompts. Machines created from the app already have it.

## Driver notice

If the machine's NVIDIA driver differs from the host's, a notice says so. It updates on its own the next time you start the machine from the app, or right away with **Update now** or **Shut down and update**.

## More options

The **⋯** menu has:

- **Start without opening**.
- **Shut down** (orderly; when Windows has updates waiting it installs them first, which can take a few minutes, and the button says so), **Force shutdown** (click twice to confirm; unsaved work is lost), **Restart**, **Pause** / **Resume**.
- **View Hyper-V console**. It only shows the basic Hyper-V display, not what the GPU draws.
- **Pair the viewer again**.
- **Update GPU driver**, or **Shut down and update driver** if it's running.
- **Create template**, or **Shut down and create template** if it's running: saves this machine's Windows as a [template](#templates). The machine stays as it is.
- **Export to a file**, or **Shut down and export** if it's running ([Export and import](#export-and-import)).
- **Rename** (when it's off): the new name is checked as you type while the cube engraves it on the board's serial plate. The machine is renamed in Hyper-V with its folder and disk; its settings, the viewer's pairing, shared disks and decoration stay with it. If a step fails, what was already renamed is undone.
- **Another icon** (also by clicking the icon next to the name) and **Another banner** (three to switch between).
- **Delete machine**: removes it from Hyper-V, with **Keep the disk** or **Delete the disk too**, after a UAC confirmation. It can't be undone.

<Aside type="caution">
Don't use Remote Desktop while streaming: it takes the console session away from the GPU display.
</Aside>

## Library

The **Library** is one page with all its sections, and a bar at the top that marks the one in view. Above them, **GpuVM's space on the drive**: how much the machine disks, templates, shared disks and ISOs take, each in its color, and how much is free.

<Shot name="library" alt="The Library: GpuVM's space on the drive and the profiles as cards with a small cube" />

- **Profiles:** the built-in profiles (marked **Built-in**) and your own, as cards with a small cube of what they give. **Duplicate** starts one from another; yours can also be edited (**Edit**, with the machine it makes beside the form) and deleted. The last card, **New profile**, creates one. Each keeps processor, memory, GPU share, disk size and type (**Fixed** / **Dynamic**), Windows image, apps to install, cleanup, the use and how the picture opens (resolution, frame rate, full screen or window), on top of the built-in profile it is **Based on**. See [Profiles](/docs/profiles/).
- **Templates:** see [Templates](#templates).
- **Shared disks:** see [Shared disks](#shared-disks).
- **Machine disks:** each machine's disk and the ones left without a machine (**In use** or **Free**). **Create a machine with this disk** creates a machine again from a disk kept when its machine was deleted, with Windows and its apps as they were; free disks can be deleted (click again to confirm, then a UAC prompt; it can't be undone). Each **Prepared Windows** is Windows applied once from an ISO and edition (about 12 GB): new machines with that Windows are created by copying it, in about a minute. Deleting it frees that space; the next machine with that Windows prepares it again.
- **ISOs:** the Windows images you used, with the machines that used them, plus the ones in Downloads and Desktop. **Add ISO** or **Add to list** remembers one, and **Remove from list** forgets it without deleting the file. Remembered ISOs are offered first when creating a machine.

## Templates

A template is a machine's Windows saved as a read-only base disk, with its apps, cleanup level and password. Create one from a machine's **⋯** menu (**Create template**), or with **New template** in the Library, picking the machine whose Windows it saves and naming it. A machine created **From template** gets a disk on top of it that only stores what changes, so it is ready in seconds and takes a few GB. Each copy gets its own streaming identity (the viewer pairs with it as a new host) and its own computer name. Its disk has the template's size and can't be enlarged.

Each template card shows the machine it came from, its size and the machines built on it, and creates a machine from it. A template can only be deleted when no machine is built on it. Templates are compacted, and when GpuVM brings a newer Sunshine or agent, its templates get it once, in the background; machines already made from a template keep theirs.

### Disposable machines

**Open disposable** on a template card makes a copy that is deleted with its disk when it shuts down, to try something without touching your machines. **Disposable** under **Create machine → From template** does the same. [Agents](/docs/agents/) work on disposable machines too.

## Export and import

**Export to a file** in a machine's **⋯** menu saves it as a single, compacted `.gpuvm` file. On another PC, **Create machine → Starting point → From a file** imports it; the imported machine gets its own streaming identity the first time it opens.

## Shared disks

A shared disk keeps your games or files apart from Windows and moves between machines, so a new machine doesn't download everything again. **New disk** asks for a name, a use (**Games** or **General**) and a maximum size; it only takes on the PC what you store on it.

- **Connect it** from the machine's **Machine** section: **Connect**, also while the machine runs. Inside, it gets a drive letter. A **Games** disk gets `SteamLibrary`, added to Steam's libraries (GpuVM closes Steam, adds it and opens it again; if a game keeps Steam open, it says so). **Disconnect** closes it inside Windows first; if a program is using it, it tells you instead. A disk attached to another machine that is off can be moved with **Bring from**.
- **Import folder** copies a folder of this PC into the disk, with its progress on the button. A Steam library brings only its games (`steamapps`). It works while the disk isn't attached to a running machine. GpuVM's own folders, Windows and folders that contain them (such as a drive or the Users folder) can't be imported.

<Aside type="note">
The copy only goes from this PC to the disk. Before writing, GpuVM checks that the disk has no links anywhere it would write (a machine could have left one to redirect the copy out of the disk); if it finds one, or a folder it can't check, it doesn't copy. A disk another machine wrote can still hold files that machine left: treat it like any disk you plug in.
</Aside>

## Settings

**Settings**, at the foot of the sidebar, has its sections on the left, their options in the middle and a live preview of the theme and accent on the right.

<Shot name="settings" alt="Settings: the Appearance section with theme, language and accent color, and the live preview of a machine" />

- **Appearance:**
  - **Theme:** **System** (the default: follows Windows), **Light** or **Dark**.
  - **Language:** **Windows** (the default: Spanish when Windows is in Spanish, English otherwise), **Español** or **English**. Every screen, the tray menu and what GpuVM reports (steps, warnings and errors) switch at once.
  - **Accent color:** blue, violet, teal, green or amber, each shown half light, half dark. It colors buttons, selection, usage bars, the logo and the machines' hologram.
  - **Animation smoothness:** the frames per second of the holograms and scenes: 15, 30, 60, 120 or **Max** (the default: as many as the monitor shows). Fewer leave more of the GPU to the machines.
  - **Animation quality:** the resolution they are drawn at, from 25 to 100 % (the default). Lower looks less sharp and uses less GPU.
  - **Status notices:** where the [status notch](#status-notch) goes.
- **Window:**
  - **When closing the window:** **Quit completely** (the default) or **Stay in the tray**: closing the window hides it and GpuVM keeps running next to the clock, so jobs in progress continue. The tray icon's menu has **Open GpuVM** and **Quit**; a left click on it opens the window again.
  - **When quitting GpuVM:** **Leave them on** (the default) or **Shut them down**: quitting shuts down every running GpuVM machine cleanly, like the Start menu inside Windows, and frees its memory. GpuVM closes at once; the shutdown continues in the background.
  - **Going back to Home from a machine:** **Keep it connected** (the default: opening it again goes straight back to its picture, and meanwhile it uses a little of this PC's GPU) or **Disconnect it**.
- **Machines** (with more than one monitor): **Open machines on** Windows' main monitor (the default) or a monitor by its name. Each machine can have its own in its options. In full screen, the machine's picture goes to that monitor.
- **Agents:** **Take control**, **Time to answer** and **Session limit**. See [Agents](/docs/agents/).
- **Captures:** the screenshots and videos folders and **Save the last 30 s** ([Captures](#captures)).
- **Service**, the Windows service that does the Hyper-V work for the app:
  - **With GpuVM** (the default): it starts when GpuVM opens and stops when it quits, once what it was doing is finished (shutting the machines down, a machine being created).
  - **With Windows**: it starts with Windows.
  - **Manual**: it only starts and stops with the **Start** and **Stop** buttons here.
  - Switching to or from **With Windows** asks for a UAC confirmation, because it changes the service's settings in Windows. Starting and stopping it doesn't. **Status** shows its state.
  - **Diagnostics report → Save report** saves a text file in Downloads with the app's and the service's logs, to look at and attach when reporting a problem. Every error GpuVM shows, or gets back from the service, is written to the app's log with its time.

GpuVM runs as a single instance: opening it again (for example from the Start menu while it's in the tray) shows the window that is already open.
